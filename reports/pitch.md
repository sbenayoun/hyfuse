## Slide 1 — The problem (why it matters)

**Combining SWIR and TIR HyLogger results is an unsolved problem (GSWA).**

- The two sensors see different minerals. A rock that is 97% quartz / 3% chlorite
  reads **100% quartz in TIR** but **100% chlorite in SWIR**.
- A good way to combine them "would be a powerful tool" — but is a *view-aware
  fusion* actually better than just using one view?

> **Our question:** does fusing SWIR + TIR recover geological domains and
> gold-related properties better than either alone — on drillholes the method
> has never seen?

*(Speaker: cite the GSWA FAQ; this framing is theirs.)*

## Slide 2 — What we built (one diagram)

**A reproducible pipeline + viewer, and a four-arm ablation.**

`fetch (THREDDS) → QC (Final Mask, units, below-detection) → 5 m windows (DuckDB)
→ per-view compositional features → domaining (PCA→HDBSCAN→ruptures) → score on
HELD-OUT holes`

Four arms, identical windows: **SWIR only · TIR only · naive concat · fused
(view-aware)**. Scored by boundary agreement vs logged lithology (permutation
baseline) and gold prediction (leave-holes-out CV).

## Slide 3 — How we tested it (the trust story)

- **Pre-registered** success criteria, tolerance, CV scheme — frozen in git
  *before* any results.
- **Leave-holes-out** CV + permutation baseline + **hole-level bootstrap**.
- **Planted-truth** simulation (known answer) and **corruption tests** (×1000
  units, depth offset, ignored mask, shuffled IDs) that must fail loudly.
- **4 independent reviews** in fresh sessions + a **calibration** that planted 3
  defects and confirmed the reviewer caught **3/3**.

> We built this to give a *trustworthy* answer — positive or not.

---

## Slide 4 — The result (stated plainly)

**On 15 gold holes: INCONCLUSIVE. Fusion is no better than the best single view.**

| arm | boundary F1 | gold Au R² (held-out) |
|---|---:|---:|
| SWIR only | 0.102 | no skill |
| TIR only | 0.139 | no skill |
| naive concat | 0.129 | no skill |
| **fused** | **0.140** | no skill |

- Fused − best single (TIR): **+0.001**, 95% CI **[−0.017, +0.022]** → includes
  zero. No reliable difference.
- **No arm predicts gold** on held-out holes (all median R² ≤ 0).

*(This is a negative result, reported as plainly as a positive one would be.)*

---

## Slide 5 — What we checked, and the limits

- Domains are **not** explained by processing level (confounder check).
- Planted-truth agreed: on known-answer data, TIR alone matched any fusion.
- **Limits (honest):** n = 15, 11/15 one deposit (Bannockburn) + 4 regional —
  weak generality; assays are selection-biased; not yet geologically validated
  (does a boundary track weathering, not lithology?).

> Anomalies here are "evidence worth investigating", never mineralisation.

---

## Slide 6 — What GSWA gets, and next steps

- A **reproducible pipeline** (`make fetch → evaluate`, bit-for-bit) + config +
  an interactive viewer, public and MIT-licensed.
- A **method result**: for these holes, a simple TIR view is as good as fusion,
  and mineralogy alone doesn't predict gold across holes.
- **Next:** more holes across *different* deposits; expert-labelled domains;
  test whether fusion helps where SWIR and TIR genuinely disagree (the chlorite
  bridge); assay-anchored calibration.
